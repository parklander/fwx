About FWX

A fire weather and fire readiness tool for Canadian Armed Forces training areas, built at CFB Shilo.

This is a prototype. It is not an official DND, CAF or CFFM product, and it is not monitored around the clock. It brings together public forecasts and fire information to support decisions. It does not replace official forecasts and alerts, the Base Fire Chief, range control or the fire danger and training restrictions in force at your base.

What it's for

Range control, base fire halls and fire planners need to know, in one place and early enough to act:

  • how dangerous fire conditions are today and over the next few days at their training area;
  • whether Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) has an alert in effect, such as a strong cold front or high winds;
  • when wind, humidity and temperature turn against them during the day: wind shifts, gusts, crossover and 30/30/30 hours;
  • whether there are fires or satellite heat detections nearby, or smoke on the way;
  • when conditions fit a prescribed burn's limits.

What's on the site

Map
Fire danger and fire weather maps, wind, precipitation and smoke forecasts, fire detections, and a forecast panel for each base with hourly conditions and burn windows.
Forecast PDF
A printable briefing: a fire danger calendar and daily values for the base.
Alert panel
A wall display for range control or the fire hall. It shows ECCC alerts for the base, lets staff acknowledge them, and shows the next hours of wind, fire danger, nearby fires and smoke.
Notifications
Your phone or computer can get a notification when ECCC issues a new alert for the bases you pick (Layers → Notify me).
Beta
A test layout that starts from a single status for the base and a 48-hour timeline.

Where the information comes from

All of it is public, open data. No DND data is used.

  • Fire danger and Fire Weather Index (FWI) codes: Canadian Wildland Fire Information System (CWFIS, Natural Resources Canada), daily.
  • Alerts, wind, precipitation and satellite images: Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC alerts, the RDPS model and GOES imagery through ECCC GeoMet).
  • Hourly point forecast: the ECCC GEM models, through Open-Meteo.
  • Fire detections: NASA FIRMS (VIIRS and MODIS satellite hotspots) and fires reported to CWFIS by the provinces and territories.
  • Smoke: ECCC FireWork, the ECCC Environmental Emergency Response (EER) smoke plume model, and BlueSky Canada.

What FWX works out itself

  • Hourly FWI: calculated from the hourly forecast, starting from CWFIS's daily codes. It shows when in the day conditions peak. It is not an official rating. The official daily fire danger class is always CWFIS's.
  • Burn windows: forecast hours compared with prescription limits. The limits can be adjusted and currently follow CFFM recommendations.
  • Crossover and 30/30/30: hours when relative humidity (%) falls to or below the temperature (°C), and when temperature is 30 °C or more, RH is 30% or less and wind is 30 km/h or more.
  • Smoke outlook: the highest forecast near-ground fine particulate (PM2.5) over the base. 35 µg/m³ counts as elevated and 100 as high.
  • Base status (beta): draft rules that combine the above. They are still being tested.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Forecasts can be wrong, and models disagree, especially for wind shifts and thunderstorms. Check what you see on the ground.
  • A satellite hotspot is a heat signal. It may be a range fire, a prescribed burn, a flare or a wildfire, and small or brief fires are often missed.
  • Each feed can be late or unavailable. Each panel shows when its data was last updated.
  • The site runs on one small server. If it is down, use the original sources above.

What this site records

To learn which features are useful, FWX logs page views with the time, your IP address, the page, the base you looked at, which map layers were on, which features were used, how long the page was on screen, a short device type (for example "Windows Edge") and any feedback you send. The log is kept for about four months, is used only to improve the tool and is not shared. There are no accounts and no tracking cookies. Your settings, such as your base and layers, are saved in your own browser.
